## 1. Overview & Diagnosis

### What is Asthma?

Asthma is a common condition that affects the airways in and out of your lungs. These airways can become sensitive and inflamed, making it harder to breathe and causing symptoms such as:

- Coughing
- Wheezing
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness

> **Airway Mechanism:** Sensitive airways cause triggers to tighten surrounding muscles, narrowing the airway. Simultaneous airway lining inflammation leads to further narrowing and increased sputum production.

### Diagnosis

There is **no single test** to diagnose asthma. Diagnosis is made based on:

1. Your symptom history
2. Results of breathing tests

## 2. Causes & Triggers

### Why Do People Get Asthma?

While often starting in childhood, asthma can develop at any stage of life. Key risk factors include:

| Risk Factor Category | Specific Factors / Details |
|---|---|
| **Genetics & Family** | Runs in families due to genetics and living environment. |
| **Medical Conditions** | Allergies, premature birth, low birth weight, bronchiolitis, and croup. |
| **Environmental & Work** | Occupational exposure to dust/chemicals, smoking, second-hand smoke, and air pollution. |
| **Hormonal Factors** | Post-puberty incidence is higher in girls than boys; late-onset asthma can develop during or after menopause. |

### Common Triggers

Anything that irritates or inflames airways can trigger symptoms:

- Colds and viruses
- Allergies, such as animal hair and pollen
- Dust, pollution, and smoke
- Stress or heightened emotions
- Temperature extremes or sudden changes
- Hormones
- Strong smells, such as perfumes, aerosols, and cleaning products

**Note on Exercise:** Some people experience exercise-induced asthma, which can often be prevented by taking a reliever medication before exercise.

## 3. Treatments & Control

Asthma is generally a lifelong condition with no cure, but effective treatments allow most people to remain symptom-free.

### Inhaler Types

| Inhaler Category | Primary Function | Active Component / Mechanism | Usage Instructions | Potential Side Effects & Advice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Preventer** | Stops symptoms from occurring. | Inhaled steroid; controls airway inflammation. | Must be taken **every day**, even when symptom-free. | Doses are low with minimal side effects. Can cause mouth soreness; rinse mouth after use to prevent it. |
| **Reliever** | Relieves active symptoms such as wheeze, chest tightness, and breathlessness. | Short-Acting Beta Agonists (SABA); quickly relaxes airway muscles. | Taken **as needed** as soon as symptoms occur. | Should not be taken routinely. |

### Inhaler Technique

Proper technique is essential to benefit from medication. Your healthcare provider should assess your technique regularly. Additional treatments can be added if inhalers are insufficient.

### Well-Controlled Asthma Criteria

Your asthma is considered well-controlled if:

- You do not require your reliever inhaler more than **3 times per week**.
- Your asthma does not interfere with normal daily activities.
- You do not wake up at night with asthma symptoms.
- You haven't had a recent asthma attack.

## 4. Severity & Action Plans

### Is Asthma Serious?

- **General Management:** Most cases are managed with daily preventer and occasional reliever inhalers.
- **Severe Asthma:** Approximately 5% of patients have severe asthma, making symptoms hard to control even on high-level treatment.

> ⚠️ **CRITICAL WARNING:** Even if your asthma is considered mild, you can still experience a severe or life-threatening attack. Always take medications as prescribed, even when feeling well. Follow a written asthma action plan provided by your asthma nurse.

## 5. Emergency Protocol: Asthma Attacks

### Signs of an Asthma Attack

According to Asthma and Lung UK, warning signs include:

1. Reliever inhaler isn't helping, or is needed more frequently than every 4 hours.
2. Symptoms such as wheeze, cough, chest tightness, and breathlessness are worsening.
3. Breathlessness makes speaking, eating, or sleeping difficult.
4. Breathing is accelerating and you cannot catch your breath properly.

### Emergency Action Steps

1. **Sit Up Straight:** Sit upright, do not lie down, and stay calm.
2. **Take Reliever:** Take **1 puff** of your reliever inhaler every **30–60 seconds**, up to a maximum of **10 puffs**.
3. **Call Emergency Services:** If you feel worse at any point or do not feel better after 10 puffs, call **999** for an ambulance immediately.
4. **Repeat Treatment:** If waiting for an ambulance, repeat Step 2 after **15 minutes**.
